Visible-light photoprotection endpoints in melasma: what the trials actually measured

Article type: Review (narrative / scoping) · Primary target: Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology → Photodermatology, Photoimmunology & Photomedicine → International Journal of Dermatology Reporting checklist: PRISMA-ScR if run as a scoping review; none if kept narrative Editor pre-pitch required: recommended — JCD accepts unsolicited reviews, but both fallbacks are "check with editor"

Drafted abstract

Sized to Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology: unstructured, 250-word cap (cap unverified — the JCD Review row in journals.csv is TBD and guidelines_last_checked is blank). The results sentence is an anticipated synthesis outcome, not collected data.

Tinted and antioxidant-supplemented sunscreens are now routinely tested in melasma for protection across the visible-light (VL) and high-energy visible spectrum, but the outcomes these trials report vary widely, which makes their results hard to compare or pool. This review maps the efficacy endpoints used in randomized and controlled melasma studies whose interventions targeted VL, including iron-oxide-tinted formulations, and asks what was actually measured: which severity indices (mMASI and its variants), which instrumental readouts (reflectance colorimetry, melanin and erythema indices, diffuse reflectance spectroscopy), which investigator and patient global assessments, and which quality-of-life instruments; how "visible-light protection" was characterized for the products used, if at all; whether pigment-specific or VL-specific endpoints were pre-specified; and how follow-up duration, seasonality, and concurrent depigmenting therapy were handled. We anticipate finding substantial heterogeneity, frequent reliance on mMASI without instrumental confirmation, inconsistent or absent reporting of the VL attenuation of the tested sunscreen, and few designs able to isolate the VL contribution from broad-spectrum UV protection. We propose a minimum endpoint set and reporting items for future melasma photoprotection trials, aligned with existing pigmentary-disorder outcome work, so tinted and VL-active products can be judged on common ground. The synthesis is limited to published literature and generates no new patient data.

Word count: ~235 / 250

Why this matters

  • For dermatology: gives clinicians and trialists a common yardstick for whether a tinted or VL-active sunscreen actually helps melasma, and exposes where current "visible-light protection" claims rest on endpoints that never measured pigment.
  • For the sun-care industry: clarifies which in-vivo endpoints and VL-attenuation disclosures a melasma efficacy claim needs to survive review, informing formulation choices (iron-oxide load, tint-shade range) and test-protocol design ahead of tighter regulator and retailer expectations.

Draft-readiness notes

  • Evidence on hand: literature only.
  • Still to pull before drafting: systematic search of melasma RCT/CCT with a VL or tinted-sunscreen arm (PubMed + Embase + trial registries); prior melasma / pigmentary-disorder core-outcome-set work to anchor the proposed endpoint list (verify what exists); any recent review of VL photoprotection endpoints, to confirm the gap.
  • Known competing work: check PROSPERO and medRxiv for a registered melasma-photoprotection endpoint or scoping review; scan <2-year narrative reviews of VL in melasma to confirm none already catalogues endpoints (scoring criterion 6 = 3, so assume moderate risk until checked).
  • Budget to lock (journals.csv): body words TBD · refs TBD · figs+tables TBD — fetch the JCD Instructions for Authors and stamp guidelines_last_checked before drafting.
  • Fallback chain: Photodermatology, Photoimmunology & Photomedicine (pre-pitch the editor) then International Journal of Dermatology (pre-pitch) — all Wiley / ScholarOne, Vancouver superscript, so reformatting between them is minimal.
